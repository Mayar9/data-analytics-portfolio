# data-analytics-portfolio

### Bank Churn Analysis 
A Bank Customer Churn dataset (typically 10,000 rows, 14 attributes covering Credit Score, Geography, Gender, Age, Tenure, Balance, NumOfProducts, HasCrCard, IsActiveMember, and Exited). 

Conducting an exploratory data analysis to identify key drivers of customer attrition and retention risks. Discovering **critical behavioral patterns**, highlighting that middle-aged cohorts, female customers, and inactive members exhibited the **highest churn risk**, with Germany recording the **highest regional churn** concentration.

Uncovered **high-value customer loss trends across tenure brackets, providing actionable insights into account engagement deficiencies and customer lifetime value risks.

## Insights:
1. **Geographic Trend**: Germany consistently exhibits a disproportionately high churn rate compared to France and Spain, despite having fewer total customers than France. 
2. **Demographics (Gender & Age)**:
  a. **Gender**: Female churn rates in this dataset are notably higher in absolute or percentage terms compared to males
  b. **Age**: Middle-aged customers (typically peak earning/borrowing years, e.g., 40–60) show a higher propensity to churn or display specific risk profiles compared to younger cohorts.
3. Active membership status (IsActiveMember) is one of the strongest predictive drivers of retention in this dataset. 
  **Engagement & Balance**:
      For Churners, Inactive customers are almost double active. And, overall around 48% of the whole dataset are inactive; this is a significant percentage that shows  lack of engagement alongside high-value balance correlations.

## Recommendations: 
- Implementing tiered loyalty programs for middle-aged tiers.
- Localized retention offers in Germany.
- Automated re-engagement workflows for inactive accounts.
