# Bank Churn Analysis 

Overview: A Bank Customer Churn dataset (typically 10,000 rows, 14 attributes covering Credit Score, Geography, Gender, Age, Tenure, Balance, NumOfProducts, HasCrCard, IsActiveMember, and Exited). 
Conducted an exploratory data analysis on a portfolio of 10,000+ bank customers to identify key drivers of customer attrition and retention risks.
Discovered critical behavioral patterns, highlighting that middle-aged cohorts, female customers, and inactive members exhibited the highest churn risk, with Germany recording the highest regional churn concentration.
Uncovered high-value customer loss trends across tenure brackets, providing actionable insights into account engagement deficiencies and customer lifetime value risks.

## Insights:
- Geographic Trend: Germany consistently exhibits a disproportionately high churn rate compared to France and Spain, despite having fewer total customers than France. 
- Demographics (Gender & Age):
    - Gender: Female churn rates in this dataset are notably higher in absolute or percentage terms compared to males
    - Age: Middle-aged customers (typically peak earning/borrowing years, e.g., 40–60) show a higher propensity to churn or display specific risk profiles compared to younger cohorts.
- Active membership status (IsActiveMember) is one of the strongest predictive drivers of retention in this dataset. 
    - Engagement & Balance:
        - For Churners, Inactive customers are almost double active. And, overall around 48% of the whole dataset are inactive; this is a significant percentage that shows  lack of engagement alongside high-value balance correlations.
- High-Value Exposure: Analysis of average balances across tenure revealed that the bank was losing high-value assets during critical tenure windows.

## Recommendations: 
- Implementing tiered loyalty programs for middle-aged tiers.
- Localized retention offers in Germany.
- Automated re-engagement workflows for inactive accounts.
