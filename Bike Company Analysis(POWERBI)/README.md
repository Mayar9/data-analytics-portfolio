# Bike Company Performance & Customer Insights Analysis
## Objectives:
This analysis leverages a normalized dataset for a Bike manufacturing and retail operations within 3 years (2020-2022) to provide high-level management with actionable data-driven insights by
evaluating Financial performance
- understanding Customer Purchasing Behavior
- assessing Product-Level profitability & performance
- identifying geographic sales trends to guide strategic decision-making

## Insights
- Product Portfolio Gap
The Findings ⟶ Zero Orders in the "Bike Components" Category
The Strategic Dilemma ⟶ we need to evaluate whether to diversify and push this category or remove it entirely to streamline our inventory
Recommendations ⟶ Conduct a quick portfolio audit. If components are required to support after-sales service or warranties, we should keep a high-turnover selection. Otherwise, phase them out to free up working capital and warehouse space, redirecting those resources toward high-performing categories.

- Revenue Concentration: Bikes Drive the Top Line
The Findings ⟶ Bikes generate the highest revenue overall, driven primarily by their high price points compared to accessories and clothing.
The Strategic Dilemma ⟶ While high-ticket bike sales keep the top line strong, relying too heavily on big-ticket items can make our revenue streams volatile (incoming money will become unpredictable, unstable, and prone to sharp ups and downs, instead of a steady, reliable flow of cash, your revenue will fluctuate wildly from month to month or year to year)
Recommendations ⟶ Build cross-selling and bundling strategies at checkout (e.g., pairing bike purchases with discounted accessories or maintenance packages) to increase Average Order Value (AOV) and build more consistent ancillary revenue.

- Regional Disparity: Strong US Footprint vs. Weak German Market Penetration
The Findings ⟶ The United States leads with the highest number of orders, whereas Germany records the lowest
The Strategic Dilemma ⟶ Entering or expanding in the German market requires a localized approach rather than a blanket strategy (an undifferentiated approach) (a broad promotional method where a business broadcasts a single, uniform message to an entire market or geographic area, rather than tailoring campaigns to specific consumer segments)
Recommendations ⟶ Launch a targeted market research initiative for Germany. We should analyze local cycling culture, consumer purchasing behaviors, competitive pricing, and macro-environmental factors to understand why traction is low before spending heavily on acquisition campaigns

- Customer Retention Warning: Declining ARPU Trend
The Findings ⟶ The Average Revenue Per User (ARPU) is on a downward trend over time, signaling underlying issues with customer retention or satisfaction.
The Strategic Dilemma ⟶ Acquiring new customers is costing us, but if they aren't sticking around or spending more over time, our long-term growth will stall.
Recommendations ⟶ Implement a post-purchase engagement loop. Introduce loyalty rewards, automated maintenance reminders, and personalized email campaigns to bring past buyers back into the ecosystem and boost lifetime value (LTV).

## Recommendations
- Financial Expansion & Market Optimization: Reallocate marketing budget away from underperforming segments (like the German market until local research is complete) and double down on high-converting US regions.
- Product Strategy Refinement: Clear out dead stock in inactive categories and refocus merchandising efforts on high-margin bike models and high-attachment accessories.
- Retention Overhaul: Task the marketing and customer success teams with diagnosing the ARPU decline through customer feedback surveys and churn analysis.
